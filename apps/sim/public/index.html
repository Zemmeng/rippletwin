    Data sources and assumptions
    • Cars, bikes and trams: Department of Transport and Planning (Victoria) Traffic Signal Volume Data (SCATS), site 2921 Swanston St / La Trobe St, 2026-08-01 to 09-27, averaged by hour for weekdays and weekends. The 25 Sep public holiday and 31 Aug (missing from the official data) are excluded. Licence CC BY 4.0.
    • Detectors used: 7 = La Trobe eastbound cars, 5 = westbound cars, 3 = Swanston north approach cars, 1 = Swanston northbound bikes, 4 = La Trobe westbound bikes, 11 / 13 = Swanston southbound / northbound trams, 22 = La Trobe westbound trams.
    • Pedestrians: City of Melbourne Pedestrian Counting System, sensors 3 (Melbourne Central), 66 (QV2, 300 Swanston St), 62 (La Trobe St North) and 187 (RMIT Building 22).
    • Filled in by assumption where there is no data: La Trobe eastbound bikes (detector 6 is faulty) use the westbound counts; Swanston southbound bikes have no detector and use the northbound counts; La Trobe eastbound trams use the westbound counts; the south footpath of La Trobe St uses the north footpath counts.
    • Signal timing is simplified from the phasing diagram in the site's configuration sheet into three phases on a 100 s cycle: phase A, Swanston through, 30 s green; phase B, exclusive Swanston left turn, about 12 s (left turners keep their green from 0 to 45 s); phase C, La Trobe, 44 s green. The east crosswalk, which left turners cross, walks only during phase A. The real junction is SCATS-adaptive; the timings are assumed.
    • All cars on the Swanston north approach are treated as turning left into La Trobe St eastbound; turning traffic on La Trobe St is not modelled.
    • Trams arrive at timetable intervals (mean headway ±25%) and dwell 8–18 s at stops, which are all on the far side of the junction. At their first corner, 60% of pedestrians cross straight ahead, 25% turn without crossing and 15% turn and cross the other street. 10% of left-turning drivers do not look properly and give way only to someone who will reach the conflict area within 0.8 s; so does any left turner who has waited more than 15 s in the junction.
    • Vehicles use the IDM car-following model. Conflicts are measured with PET (the time between two road users passing through the same conflict area) and TTC (seconds until catching up with the vehicle ahead at current speeds), with a 1.5 s threshold; under 0.5 s counts as severe.
