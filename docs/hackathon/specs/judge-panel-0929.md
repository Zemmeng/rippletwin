# Simulated judge panel: RippleTwin UI review (29 Sep 20:15, live build e84aec5)

*Archived from the hackathon (29 Sep – 1 Oct 2026). Translated from the original Chinese; superseded details are kept as they were.*

> **This is not feedback from the real judges.** It is a simulated review written by three AI agents (Claude Opus) that were each asked to role-play one judge. The team used it internally as a stress test of the 29 Sep UI, and it led to the fixes in [`T20-addendum.md`](T20-addendum.md). The UI it critiques no longer exists.

Three Opus agents each played one judge. Each looked at screenshots of the 4 steps, the page inventory, the scoring criteria and the 7-minute demo length, and gave independent comments. All three found it "too complicated".

## Industry judge (traffic management operations manager at RPM Hire)

**Biggest scoring risk:** credibility collapses in Q&A, dragging down both "effectiveness 40%" and "feasibility 30%". Right now two works sites share the screen: Lonsdale at 08:00 is the real engine, while La Trobe × Swanston at 17:00 is a scripted simulation, wrapped in a safety score, an AI red team, 840 decisions/s and similar packaging. A judge who knows traffic only has to ask "what is the safety score for this Lonsdale plan?" or "move the barrier 10 m and run the stress test again", and the only answer is "that is preset". That one moment makes even the real engine numbers, such as 918 m and +509 s, look suspect. The second risk is that the core selling point of the advice card, "SAVE 9 MIN", relies on 61% of drivers following the VMS detour. Anyone who works on site knows the real compliance rate is far lower, so without the assumption stated it will be read as overly optimistic. So: cut all the preset micro-simulation and decoration, keep only one works site and one real-engine storyline, and put the key assumptions on the table.

**Confusing in the first 30 seconds:**
- The right-hand panel says "Lonsdale St westbound closure 08:00", but the map shows the La Trobe × Swanston junction, with annotations such as barrier B-12 40 m, the route 250 bus stop, passage only 1.1 m and ADI 0.029 km². The judge's first question will be: which works are we actually planning?
- The 6 unlabelled icons in the left bar, the 6 weather buttons at the top, the imagery / near-infrared / street switch, the lat/lon probe and the graticule together look like a satellite remote-sensing dashboard, not a traffic management plan (TMP) tool. The judges cannot tell where to look
- The timeline at the bottom says 17:00–18:00, with a conflict histogram, but the plan is set for 08:00. The times contradict each other, and it is unclear what the timeline is for
- The top-bar "safety score" shows – / 100 in step 1, 42 in step 2 and 86 in step 4. Whose score is it? What does it have to do with the Lonsdale plan? There is no way to answer that on the spot
- The compact view of step 1 only enlarges the VMS and the signs. The queue in metres and the extra seconds per vehicle are folded into the row "Engine · real CBD traffic 08:00 >", so after clicking through the plan the judges still cannot see the impact
- Too much jargon for anyone to parse in 30 seconds: stress test / ripple trace / AI red team / 250 agents × 30 behaviour variants · random seed 4218 / 840 agent decisions/s / TTC < 1.5 s / ADI. "AI red team" also implies an LLM is in use, when the sign reading is actually rule-based
- The units in step 4 don't match: the advice card says "−1,580 veh·h" (summed over the whole works period), while the table below is hourly "veh·min" (10,493 → 966). The judges will think it is a calculation error
- The step 3 map does not frame Lonsdale's 918 m queue line; it stays on the La Trobe junction and even says "LA TROBE ST +17 veh·min", which does not match the heading
- The UI defaults to Chinese, and the advice card has an English sentence mixed in ("Name the fastest detour…"). Projected for English-speaking judges, that will look messy

**Suggest deleting:**
- The weather switcher (6 types) + the bottom-left weather legend "Clear · baseline" + the weather layer in the left bar: all simulated, none of it affects any engine number. Traffic plans do not look at radar, SAR flooding or land-surface temperature. The moment a judge asks "is this real data?", it only lowers credibility
- The imagery / near-infrared (NIR) basemaps: they are procedurally drawn. Saying "near-infrared" implies real satellite data, which will be embarrassing under questioning in Q&A. Keep only the street basemap
- The graticule + the lat/lon probe + the degree-minute-second labels around the map: GIS decoration with no value for a works plan; it takes up screen space and distracts
- The top-bar "safety score": it comes from a preset micro-simulation of a different junction, has nothing to do with the Lonsdale plan, and nobody can say what it scores
- The conflict histogram on the bottom timeline (17:00–18:00): it contradicts the 08:00 plan and its data is preset. Time should be controlled only by the hour buttons in step 1
- The whole "02 Stress test" step (variants 22/30, 840 decisions/s, active agents, conflicts, severe, harsh braking, variant list): scripted, and at a different works site. A judge only has to say "move the barrier and run it again" and it falls apart, taking the credibility of the real engine numbers with it (feasibility is 30%)
- The lower half of step 4, "junction safety repair" (safety score ring, Δ1–Δ4, preset before/after table, live sample, copy plan, left/right slider comparison): preset data at a different site; it doubles the length of step 4 and dilutes the real conclusion, "use Russell and save 9 minutes"
- The three rows in step 1, "junction micro-model (4 items)", "road users on the map: 61 live" and "stress-test weather": all part of the micro-simulation that is being cut, and unrelated to the current plan
- The "Run AI red team test →" button and "250 agents × 30 behaviour variants · random seed 4218": misleading (the LLM is not switched on), and they lead people to fake data. Replace with "See the impact →", jumping straight to the network ripple
- The La Trobe junction map annotations in the engine flow (barrier B-12, route 250 bus stop, passage 1.1 m, ADI analysis area, bike lane offset 11 m): that is a different works site, and showing it alongside the Lonsdale plan is the root of the "two works areas" problem
- The light/dark switch: not needed for the demo. Use the light theme on the projector

**Merge / hide:**
- The 4 steps at the top: make them 3, in words a judge understands at once: 1 Plan the works / 2 See the impact / 3 Fix the plan. No more "stress test", "ripple trace" or "repair"
- The engine number cards (queue, extra wait per vehicle, detour, network delay, affected trams and buses, pedestrians detouring): take them out of the collapsed row and put them at the top of the step 1 compact view as 4–6 big numbers. They jump the moment you switch between one lane and full closure, change the hour or close the footpath. That is the "aha" of the whole pitch

The compact view keeps only a "data sources" badge that opens a pop-up: SCATS, PTV timetables, City of Melbourne pedestrian counters; 1,127 of the 1,513 road sections are interpolated and labelled "estimate"
- The sign rule-check hints: merge them under the VMS and signs as a single ✓/⚠ line, for example "distance 100 m complies"
- The units of the savings in step 4: make them one unit. Use "veh·min per hour" in both the advice card and the table, or "veh·h over the whole works period" in both, and state how many hours the works period is counted as
- The premise of the detour share 14% → 61%: state "assumes X% of drivers follow the VMS detour" in the sources pop-up. My experience on site is that real compliance is far below 60%; stating the assumption is better than having a judge expose it
- The Chinese/English switch: default to English for the demo; keep the switch but make it smaller. Clean up the mixed Chinese and English in the advice card
